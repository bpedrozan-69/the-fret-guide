How Fret works

A guitar fretboard, a chord identifier, a song builder, a chord-sheet reader and an ear trainer. No account, no ads, no server. Everything you make stays on the device you made it on.

Start here

Two things to know before you build anything you'd be sad to lose.

Your work lives in this browser

There is no account and nothing is uploaded. Songs, chord sheets, saved chords, drill scores and settings are stored by the browser you're using. That's why the app is fast and works with the internet off — and it's why the same songs won't appear on your phone if you made them on a laptop.

Clearing your browsing data erases everything. Refreshing, closing the tab and restarting the device are all safe.

o

Back up now, not later. Every page has Back up all in the sidebar. It downloads one file holding your songs, sheets, saved chords and scores. Restore reads it back and asks whether to merge or replace before touching anything.

That file is also how you move everything to another device — email it to yourself and restore on the other end.

Muted, open and fretted are three different things

Every string is in one of three states, and the app treats them as genuinely distinct. A muted string contributes nothing to the chord; an open string sounds. If a chord isn't being named the way you expect, a string is usually open when you meant it muted.

×
Muted — not played, ignored when naming the chord
o
Open — played with nothing fretted, counts towards the chord
●
Fretted at the marked fret

Lab /

The fretboard. Put your fingers somewhere and find out what you're playing, or type a chord name and find out where the fingers go.

Putting notes on the board

  • Click any fret to place a dot. Click the same fret again to clear that string back to open.
  • Click the string letter on the headstock to mute it. Click again to unmute.
  • Shift-click any fret cell to mute that string without hunting for the headstock.
  • Type a name — Am, Cadd9, F#m7, Bbmaj7, G13 — and press Show on board.

What it tells you back

The chord name appears as you play, with a plain-English note on how it sounds and a difficulty rating. Barre chords draw as a single bar with the finger number on it, rather than separate dots, because that's what your hand actually does.

o

It accepts chords with notes missing. Guitarists drop the fifth constantly. Play one and Fret still names it, with the missing note in brackets — G (no D) — instead of refusing to recognise it.

o

One note short and it offers to finish. If what you've placed is a single note away from a real chord, a card appears naming the chord and telling you exactly where to put the missing finger. Accept it or dismiss it.

Finding an easier way to play something

Another way to play it cycles through every playable voicing of the current chord, ranked so the easiest comes first. The line beside it tells you which one you're on and whether something easier exists. Easier version jumps straight to the simplest shape.

Tuner

The Tuner button below the fretboard opens a panel. Press Start listening — the microphone is only requested at that moment, never on page load — then play one string at a time. You get the note, the frequency, how many cents sharp or flat, and a needle that centres when you're right.

The six string targets follow whatever tuning and capo you've set, so with a capo on the third fret the low E target becomes G. Tap any target to hear a reference tone.

×

Microphone access needs a real click. Browsers refuse it otherwise. If you blocked it by accident you'll need to re-allow Fret in your browser's site settings — the app can't re-ask.

Saving chords

Save chord adds the current shape to the strip at the bottom. Click any saved chord to load it back. From there, Print sheet makes a clean printable page of diagrams, and Send to Strum turns the whole strip into a playable song.

Practice /practice

Five modes. Three test whether you know a chord, one builds the skill everyone gets stuck on, and one shows whether any of it is working.

Hear a chord · Hear a note · See a chord

Fret plays or draws something and you identify it from four options. Difficulty moves you from open chords through barre chords to extended shapes. After each answer you see where those notes sit on the full neck and on a piano keyboard.

o

Tap or play it. On See a chord and Hear a note there's a small toggle. Switch to Play it and the answer buttons are replaced by a microphone: play the chord on your guitar and Fret listens.

It's forgiving on purpose — any voicing, any octave. It's listening for the right notes, not the exact shape it drew. The target notes light up as it hears them, and holding a correct chord for about half a second counts it.

Chord changes

The one-minute-changes exercise, which is how guitar teachers actually fix the beginner wall. Pick two chords, hit start, and switch between them as many times as you cleanly can in sixty seconds. Tap anywhere on the big area each time you change — the highlight flips so you always know which chord you're heading to.

Scores save per pair, so next session you're chasing your own number. Only count a change if it rang cleanly; the app can't hear buzzing, so that part is on you.

Progress

Everything you've done, gathered in one place: days practised, total questions and accuracy, a bar chart of your recent drill runs with personal bests in gold, and every chord pair you've drilled ranked weakest first.

That ranking is the useful part. The pairs at the top are the ones to drill next.

×

Progress fills up slowly, and that's the point. A session is banked when you switch mode, change difficulty or leave the page. One evening of use won't tell you much. Two weeks will.

Strum /strum

Build a song out of chords, give it a tempo and a strumming pattern, and listen back. Export it as audio if you want to keep it.

Writing a song by typing it

The fastest way in is the text box. One section per line:

Verse:  Am D7 C E x2
Chorus: Cadd9 Bm G D
Bridge: Fmaj7 Em Am

The section name and the x2 are both optional. Build song replaces everything; Append adds to the end. Chords it can't read are listed rather than silently dropped.

Each section also has its own small input, so you can add to one section without retyping the rest.

Sections and repeats

A song is a list of sections, each with its own chords, repeat count and strumming pattern. Reorder them with the arrows, duplicate one with Copy, rename by clicking the title. The repeat count means a verse can play twice before the chorus without you entering it twice.

Length is measured in beats

Set the song's tempo in BPM, or tap Tap four times at the speed you want. Chord lengths are then musical — one beat, half a bar, one bar, two bars — rather than arbitrary seconds. Click a chord's length label to cycle through the options.

Click turns on a metronome with an accented downbeat. Count-in gives you a bar of clicks before playback starts.

Strumming patterns

Each section can have a pattern — eight slots to a bar, down-strokes and up-strokes. Pick a preset like Classic pop and then click any individual slot to cycle it between down, up and skip. Up-strokes genuinely sound different: they sweep from high string to low, drop the bass strings, and sit quieter.

×

Patterns are ignored on arpeggio chords. A strumming pattern on a broken chord is a contradiction, so if a pattern seems to do nothing, check whether those chords are set to Arp rather than Strum.

Editing a lot of chords at once

Select chords puts checkboxes on every chord. Pick some — or use All, or Select section in a section header — then apply a length, style, speed or delete to the whole selection. For a long imported song this is the difference between four clicks and four hundred.

The dropdown beside it applies one strumming pattern to every section at once.

Sound and export

Four tones — steel string, nylon, clean electric, and the original synth. The guitar sounds are generated by modelling a plucked string, not by playing back recordings, which is why they work offline and add nothing to the download.

WAV renders instantly. MP3 takes a moment longer. Text writes out the structure, chords, lengths and any notes you've added.

Lyrics /lyrics

Chord sheets with the chords sitting above the words, and a performance mode that scrolls them past you while both your hands are busy.

Making a sheet

Switch to Edit lyrics and type or paste the words. Switch back to Chord sheet and every word becomes clickable — click the word where a chord starts, then type the name or pick from the quick list.

Chords land exactly where you put them. Click one to change it, remove it, or nudge it a character left or right. If two chords overlap, the left one turns amber as a warning rather than silently shifting.

o

Hover a chord to see the shape. On a computer, resting the pointer on any chord name pops up a small diagram above it — barres and all.

Performance mode

Takes over the whole screen: large type, chords above words, section names picked out. Scroll starts it creeping down the page at your chosen speed, and the controls fade out while it runs.

Import /import

Paste a chord chart from anywhere and Fret works out which lines are chords, which are lyrics, and which word each chord belongs to.

Select a chart on any site, copy it, paste it in, and press Read the chart. Nothing is saved until you've seen the preview and chosen what to keep.

[Verse 1]
G                              Em
Come gather 'round people wherever you roam
C                              G
And admit that the waters around you have grown

You can save the result as a lyric sheet, a playable song, or both. The chord positions carry across exactly — a chord at the twenty-first character lands on the word at the twenty-first character.

How it decides

  • Anything in brackets — [Verse], [Chorus] — becomes a section.
  • A line where every item is a valid chord name is a chord line.
  • A lone letter like C on its own line is resolved by what follows it: chords sit above words.
  • Guitar tab lines are skipped, and the count is shown so you know they were dropped.
  • Everything else is lyrics.
×

Check the preview. Unusual spacing, chords written inline inside a lyric, and tab notation will all confuse it. The preview exists so you catch that before saving rather than after.

Shared settings

Four settings live in the Lab sidebar and follow you to every other page.

Tuning
Standard, Drop D, Open G, DADGAD, half step down, full step down
Capo
Draws a bar on the board, greys out the frets behind it, and renames every chord to what it actually sounds like
Guitar tone
Steel string, nylon, clean electric or synth — applies to every sound the app makes
Handedness
Left mirrors every fretboard in the app, with all the labels kept readable
o

The capo renames things, it doesn't just move them. Set it to the third fret and a G shape is offered as B♭, because that's the note coming out of the guitar. Practice does the same, and tells you the capo position alongside the answer.

On a narrow screen the sidebar becomes the menu button in the top corner. Each page puts something different in there — the Lab its settings, Strum your songs, Lyrics your sheets, Practice the difficulty and your scores.

Offline & install

Fret is a website that behaves like an app. Nothing it needs to run comes from the internet.

Visit it once with a connection and it caches itself. After that it opens with the wifi off — the fretboard, the chord maths, the audio, the tuner, even MP3 export. There are no API keys, no fonts fetched from elsewhere and no analytics.

Installing it

  • iPhone or iPad: Share, then Add to Home Screen.
  • Android: Chrome offers to install it, or use Add to Home Screen in the menu.
  • Computer: Chrome shows an install icon at the right of the address bar.

Installed, it opens in its own window with no browser chrome.

×

Installing does not back anything up. The installed app and the browser tab may not even share storage. Use Back up all, and use Restore on the installed copy if you want your songs there too.

Every quirk in one place

The things that surprise people, collected. Most are deliberate.

  • Nothing syncs. Laptop and phone keep separate libraries. The backup file is the bridge.
  • Clearing browsing data erases your work. Refreshing and restarting are safe.
  • Muted and open are different states. Most misnamed chords trace back to this.
  • The microphone only opens on a button press, and a blocked permission has to be undone in browser settings.
  • Listening mode is forgiving by design. Right notes, any shape, any octave.
  • Chord-change scores are on your honour. The app can't hear a buzz.
  • Strumming patterns don't apply to arpeggio chords.
  • The capo changes chord names, not just the diagram.
  • Long lines don't wrap in performance mode. Auto text size avoids it; overriding the size can bring it back.
  • The import parser can be fooled by unusual spacing or inline chords. The preview is there for that.
  • Progress needs weeks, not evenings, before it says anything useful.
  • Chords are always spelled with sharps unless the key naturally reads flat.
Fret is built to work without an account, without a subscription and without a connection. Nothing you make here is sent anywhere. Back it up and it's yours.
